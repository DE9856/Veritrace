# Evaluation

How well Veritrace's three labels match hand labels, on claims the tuning never saw. Generated by `python -m calibration.evaluate --write`; see `backend/calibration/` for the data and code.

## Setup

- **Labels:** high (a passage states the claim), inferred (follows from the passages without outside knowledge), hallucinated (contradicted, or not supported). Judged by hand against the two sample documents only.
- **Claims:** hand-written claims (`dev_claims.yaml`, `test_claims.yaml`, `fresh_claims.yaml`), plus every claim the agent extracted from its own answers to the questions in `answer_questions.yaml`, labelled in `answer_labels.yaml`.
- **Protocol:** the scoring rules were designed and the settings tuned on dev only; test and fresh are only scored. In 0.9.0 the test set's errors were read to find what to fix, so test overstates that change; the fresh set, written before it, is the cleaner measure. The numbers below use the settings in `config.yaml`; the grid search on dev (`calibration/tune.py`) is shown for comparison.

## `qwen3:4b-instruct`

Answers, judge and 2 resampled answers per question from `qwen3:4b-instruct`; NLI from `MoritzLaurer/DeBERTa-v3-base-mnli-fever-anli`.

### Confidence calibration

Is the score a probability? A claim counts as supported when its hand label is high or inferred. The score is mapped to a calibrated chance of support with Platt scaling, sigmoid(slope x score + intercept), fitted on dev (`calibration/reliability.py`). Fitted now: slope 6.27, intercept -2.07; `config.yaml` has 6.27 and -2.07. ECE is the gap between confidence and the share supported, averaged over 5 equal-width bins (lower is better), with a 95% bootstrap interval; Brier is the mean squared error of the confidence.

| Set | Claims | ECE, raw score | ECE, calibrated | Brier, raw | Brier, calibrated |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dev (fitted on) | 84 | 0.093 (0.06-0.15) | 0.024 (0.02-0.09) | 0.073 | 0.068 |
| Test | 67 | 0.090 (0.06-0.14) | 0.058 (0.04-0.10) | 0.048 | 0.031 |
| Fresh | 37 | 0.153 (0.10-0.23) | 0.081 (0.04-0.15) | 0.076 | 0.047 |

### Synthetic hallucinations

True claims with one fact changed by fixed rules (`calibration/synthetic.py`, no model): a date moved, a number or count changed, a name swapped for a confusable one from the documents, a negation flipped. Two sources: the documents' own sentences, and the hand-labelled high claims (paraphrases, as a model writes them). The unchanged claims are the controls. Consistency is off, since a synthetic claim has no question of its own to resample. Caught means labelled hallucinated.

### Ablation (test, current settings)

Each row lets the scorer use only some signals; NLI entailment, contradiction and passage match are always on. Missing weights are renormalised, as in the app.

| Setting | Accuracy | Macro-F1 | Hallucination precision | Hallucination recall | Hallucination F1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NLI only | 76% | 0.60 | 0.76 | 0.87 | 0.81 |
| NLI + novelty | 79% | 0.76 | 0.76 | 0.87 | 0.81 |
| NLI + novelty + judge | 90% | 0.86 | 0.88 | 0.93 | 0.90 |
| NLI + novelty + judge + consistency | 90% | 0.86 | 0.88 | 0.93 | 0.90 |
| Full ensemble (+ self-label) | 91% | 0.88 | 0.88 | 0.93 | 0.90 |
| Full minus judge | 79% | 0.76 | 0.76 | 0.87 | 0.81 |
| Full minus consistency | 90% | 0.86 | 0.88 | 0.93 | 0.90 |
| Full minus novelty | 82% | 0.71 | 0.88 | 0.93 | 0.90 |
| Baseline: LLM judge alone | 78% | 0.57 | 0.74 | 0.93 | 0.82 |
| Baseline: self-consistency alone | 70% | 0.51 | 0.65 | 0.87 | 0.74 |

The two baselines can only answer supported or not, so they never say inferred, which caps their macro-F1.
