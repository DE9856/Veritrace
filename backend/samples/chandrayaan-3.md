# Chandrayaan-3

Chandrayaan-3 is the third lunar exploration mission of the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). It followed Chandrayaan-2, whose Vikram lander crashed during its landing attempt in September 2019. Chandrayaan-3 carried a lander and a rover but no orbiter. Its propulsion module carried the lander and rover to lunar orbit.

## Launch and journey

Chandrayaan-3 was launched on 14 July 2023 aboard an LVM3 rocket from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre in Sriharikota. The spacecraft entered lunar orbit on 5 August 2023. The lander module separated from the propulsion module on 17 August 2023.

## Landing

The Vikram lander touched down near the lunar south pole on 23 August 2023. With this landing, India became the fourth country to achieve a soft landing on the Moon and the first to land near the lunar south pole. The landing site was later named the Shiv Shakti point, and India declared 23 August as National Space Day.

## Rover and science results

The Pragyan rover rolled out of the Vikram lander shortly after the landing and travelled about 100 metres across the surface. The Laser Induced Breakdown Spectroscope (LIBS) on the rover confirmed the presence of sulphur in the lunar surface near the south pole. It also detected aluminium, calcium, iron, chromium, titanium, manganese, silicon and oxygen.

The ChaSTE instrument on the lander measured the temperature profile of the lunar topsoil down to a depth of about 10 centimetres. On 3 September 2023 the Vikram lander performed a hop experiment, firing its engines to rise about 40 centimetres and land again about 30 to 40 centimetres away.

## End of surface operations

The lander and rover were designed to operate for about one lunar day, roughly 14 Earth days. Both were put into sleep mode in early September 2023 before the lunar night. They did not respond to wake-up attempts when sunlight returned. In November 2023 the propulsion module was moved from lunar orbit back to an orbit around the Earth.
