# James Webb Space Telescope

The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) is a space telescope built to observe the universe in infrared light. It was developed by NASA together with the European Space Agency (ESA) and the Canadian Space Agency (CSA). The telescope is named after James E. Webb, who led NASA from 1961 to 1968.

## Launch and orbit

JWST was launched on 25 December 2021 on an Ariane 5 rocket from Kourou in French Guiana. It arrived at its orbit around the second Sun-Earth Lagrange point (L2), about 1.5 million kilometres from Earth, on 24 January 2022.

## Mirror and sunshield

The primary mirror is 6.5 metres across and is made of 18 hexagonal segments of gold-coated beryllium. The gold coating improves the mirror's reflection of infrared light. A five-layer sunshield, about the size of a tennis court, protects the telescope from the heat of the Sun, the Earth and the Moon. The mirror and the sunshield were folded for launch and unfolded in space during the following weeks.

## Instruments

JWST carries four science instruments: the Near-Infrared Camera (NIRCam), the Near-Infrared Spectrograph (NIRSpec), the Mid-Infrared Instrument (MIRI), and the Near-Infrared Imager and Slitless Spectrograph (NIRISS). MIRI has to be colder than the other instruments, so a cryocooler keeps it below 7 kelvin.

## First results

The first image, Webb's First Deep Field, was shown on 11 July 2022. It showed the galaxy cluster SMACS 0723 and thousands of distant galaxies behind it. The rest of the first full-colour images were released on 12 July 2022.
